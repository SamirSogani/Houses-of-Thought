Houses of Thought runs your question through seven rooms of critical thinking, checks the work against nine standards, and keeps rebuilding until it would survive contact with reality — not just look good in the moment. Free, always.

The method

Seven rooms, one house.

Every question moves through the same seven elements of reasoning, given a physical place to live. Hover a room to see where it sits on the drawing.

  1. 1
    Frame

    Concepts, purpose, and the one question the house exists to answer — staked out before anything argues.

  2. 2
    Perspectives

    Sub-questions and points of view — the same house, a different view from each.

  3. 3
    Evidence

    What's actually known, cited, before it gets shaped into an answer.

  4. 4
    Assumptions

    What the whole thing rests on, usually out of sight.

  5. 5
    Conclusion

    Where the perspectives and evidence actually carry into one statement.

  6. 6
    Implications

    What happens when this meets the real world.

  7. 7
    Review

    Checked nine ways, stress-tested, and sent back until it holds.

Where the weight settles
Leans toward: take it — 63 / 37

Holds up

  • Salary jump covers the current gap
  • Direct path to the role you actually want
  • Team lead is someone you already trust

Doesn't

  • Longer commute eats into evenings
  • Equity vests over 4 years — the clock restarts
  • Current team is mid-project; timing is awkward

What might follow

If you take it: expect a three-month ramp before the new role feels earned. Plan the commute change before day one, not after — that's the part people underrate.
