# Brainstorm: Bold Animation & Design Ideas

[← Back to index](README.md)

**Not built. Not in the mockup. A menu to react to.** Everything else in
this folder is a real proposal for the redesign; this doc is the opposite —
deliberately wilder than anything that should ship without your say-so.
Pick favorites, veto the rest, and only then do any of it get built.

## The big swings

1. **Blueprint-to-built scroll.** The whole homepage background is one
   continuous illustration that transforms as you scroll — flat cyan
   line-drawing at the top, fully "constructed" and textured by the
   bottom. Not section-by-section; one drawing, slowly finishing itself
   under your scroll. *Risk: technically the most ambitious thing here.*

2. **Exploded house on hover.** The seven-room diagram, on hover, blows
   apart into its seven room-blocks floating in isometric space — like an
   IKEA-instruction exploded diagram — then reassembles when you look
   away. *Risk: needs real isometric/3D-ish rendering, not flat SVG.*

3. **A live storm test.** The Weather room isn't a static illustration —
   an actual animated storm plays over the house: wind, rain streaks, a
   flash of lightning, the structure visibly flexing. If it holds, the
   storm clears to sun. The most literal, most dramatic possible staging
   of "survive contact with reality."

4. **Ink-bleed page transitions.** Instead of a fade or slide, moving
   between pages looks like fountain-pen ink bleeding and spreading
   across the parchment to reveal what's underneath — wet, tactile,
   unmistakably "written," not "loaded."

## Character & voice

5. **The Inspector makes cameos.** A small hand-drawn line-art figure — a
   building inspector, clipboard and hard hat — walks across the page at
   a few key moments only (after a Mini House finishes, they walk up and
   stamp it). One recurring character, used sparingly, never a mascot
   that's always on screen.

6. **The closing question writes itself.** The reflective question that
   ends a Mini House isn't typeset — it's an animated pen stroke, drawn
   letter by letter, slowing down like someone genuinely pausing mid-
   thought. The one moment the product is proudest of (no verdict, just a
   question) gets the most human treatment on the site.

## Texture & atmosphere

7. **Dark mode = the drafting table at 2am.** Not a generic inverted
   palette — a warm pool of desk-lamp light around the content, real
   shadow at the edges. Ties the origin story (a parent working late)
   into the *feeling* of the page, not just the copy.

8. **A drafting cursor.** Inside diagrams specifically, the cursor
   becomes a small compass/pencil that leaves a brief hand-drawn trail as
   it moves — you're the one drafting, for a second.

9. **Sound, opt-in only, two sounds total.** A soft stamp-thud when an
   Inspection passes; a faint pencil-scratch under the hero's draw-in.
   Muted by default, one explicit toggle, nothing autoplays. Almost
   nobody in this category uses sound at all — even this little would be
   memorable by contrast.

## A stretch idea for `/examples`

10. **An explorable neighborhood, not a card grid.** Published example
    houses scattered across a small pannable blueprint "town," each
    house's size/detail keyed to its Strength score. Turns a gallery page
    into a diorama worth poking around in.

## A hidden one, if you want it

11. **A stress-test easter egg.** Type "why" anywhere on the site and the
    whole page gives a single honest shake, then settles, with a small
    toast: *"Still standing."* Zero cost to the real UX since it's
    hidden; pure delight for whoever finds it.
